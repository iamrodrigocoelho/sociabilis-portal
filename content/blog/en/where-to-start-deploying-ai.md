Many companies start their AI journey with the wrong question: "which tool should we use?". The question that tends to produce results is a different one: **which problem in our operation is worth solving first?**

This article brings together the criteria we use to help organizations move beyond pilots and put AI to work every day.

## Start with a problem, not a model

A good first use case comes from a concrete pain point, well known to the people who do the work. Some signs that you have found a good candidate:

- the task is repetitive and takes up the time of skilled people;
- the information needed already exists, but it is scattered across documents and systems;
- someone owns the process and is willing to test and give feedback;
- before you start, you can describe what a good result would look like.

If the idea depends on "the AI will figure out what to do on its own", it is not a use case yet. It is an intention.

## Define how you will measure success

Before writing a single line of code, agree with the team on which indicators will show that the solution works. They can be about productivity, quality, adoption or cost. What matters is that they are observable and that someone tracks them.

> Without success criteria, every pilot looks promising and none of them reaches operations.

Measuring also helps you decide, honestly, when **not** to move forward. Ending a pilot that did not deliver value is a good outcome: it frees up time and budget for the next one.

## Look at data and governance from the start

Much of applied AI depends on the company's own knowledge: policies, procedures, contracts, service history. That is why it pays to answer these questions early:

1. Where is this data, and who is responsible for it?
2. Who can access what, and does the solution need to respect that?
3. How will users know where an answer came from?

Governance is not a step at the end of the project. When it is part of the design, it prevents rework and gives you the confidence to scale.

## Choose the right model, not the biggest one

Not every problem needs the most advanced model available. Often, a combination of search over the company's documents (RAG), smaller models and simple automations solves the problem with lower cost, lower latency and less resource consumption. **Efficiency is also sustainability.**

## A simple roadmap for the first steps

- List the processes that take the most time and where information is scattered.
- Prioritize by expected impact and deployment effort.
- Choose one case, define the indicators and prototype with the people who use the process.
- Deploy it in the real workflow and track the results.
